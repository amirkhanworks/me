Build the sales process around how people decide

Amir Khan, Co-founder, Luminox Automation

Our first instinct on pricing was an engineer's instinct: ask the customer how many lights, fans and switchboards they have.

That's the right question for a quote. It's the wrong question for a buyer.

Most people don't know. They'd have to walk around the house counting. Most won't bother.

So at Luminox we changed it. You tell us how many rooms you want automated. You get a price, installation included. No counting, no site visit just to get a number.

The engineering didn't change. Same board, same relays, same install. What changed is who the question was designed for.

I see this gap in a lot of hard tech companies. The product is built around how it works. The sales process should be built around how people decide.

Three things I'd tell any technical founder:

  1. If a prospect has to do homework to get a price, most won't.
  2. Precision belongs in the installation, not the first conversation.
  3. A simple price people can act on beats a perfect one nobody asks for.

Building a technical product is hard. Making it understandable in ten seconds is harder.

What does your sales process ask that your customer can't answer?
